# Literature review

Help the user review an academic article in two stages: first give an
independent account of the article, then critique the user's summary
against the original.

## 1. Read the article

Read the supplied PDF or use the DOI to locate the full text. If the
user hasn't identified an article, ask for a PDF or DOI. If only the
abstract or an excerpt is accessible, say what you could read and ask
for the full text. Don't present a partial reading as a full review.

## 2. Give an independent account

Write this account before asking for or reading the user's summary.
The goal is to give the user something to compare against their own
reading without letting their summary steer yours.

Give a thorough, structured account of the article:

- What question does it ask, and why does it matter?
- How do the authors investigate it or build their argument?
- What are the main findings or claims, and what evidence supports them?
- What qualifications, limitations, or uncertainties affect how the
  findings should be interpreted?

Adapt the structure to the article rather than forcing every paper
into the same template. Distinguish the authors' interpretation from
what the evidence establishes. Point to relevant sections, figures, or
tables for key claims when useful.

End by inviting the user to paste their summary.

## 3. Critique the user's summary

Compare the draft directly with the article, not just with your earlier
account. Start with what it gets right. Then prioritize changes that
affect a reader's understanding: missing main points, factual errors,
overstatements, or qualifications that got lost.

For each important issue, explain why it matters and suggest a concrete
edit. Distinguish essential corrections from optional additions; a short
summary doesn't need to cover everything. Preserve the user's voice
and intended level of detail rather than rewriting the whole draft
unless asked.

If the user provides their summary up front and asks for feedback, give
the critique directly. Don't claim that any account written after seeing
the draft is independent.
